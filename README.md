# Research Ideas

A lightweight repository for capturing, developing, and evaluating research ideas.

The goal is to **store ideas early, compare them explicitly, and progressively sharpen the best ones** rather than waiting until an idea is fully formed.

The structure is inspired by Michael A. Fischbach's framework for problem choice and decision trees in science and engineering.

## Workflow

Use **GitHub Issues as the unit of a research idea**.

1. **Capture quickly** — open a `Quick Research Idea` issue as soon as an idea seems worth remembering.
2. **Develop selectively** — when an idea survives initial thought, rewrite or reopen it using the `Research Idea — Deep Evaluation` template.
3. **Test assumptions early** — identify the highest-risk assumption and the earliest go/no-go test.
4. **Update the decision tree** — keep the issue alive as the idea changes; do not treat the original formulation as sacred.
5. **Close deliberately** — close ideas that are no longer worth pursuing. A closed issue is a useful decision record, not a failure.

## Suggested issue lifecycle

`raw idea` → `promising` → `evaluating` → `active` → `completed / parked / rejected`

GitHub labels can be added later if this lifecycle becomes useful in practice. The templates intentionally work without any preconfigured labels.

## What makes a useful research-idea issue?

At minimum, it should make four things explicit:

- **Problem** — What important problem are we trying to solve?
- **Impact** — If successful, why would it matter?
- **Risk** — What is the most important uncertain assumption?
- **First test** — What is the fastest way to test that assumption?

For ideas worth deeper investment, also evaluate competitive advantage, fixed vs. floating parameters, alternative paths, and explicit go/no-go criteria.

## Templates

### Quick Research Idea

For low-friction capture. It should take only a few minutes to file.

Use this when the idea is still vague, speculative, or triggered by a paper, dataset, observation, limitation, or conversation.

### Research Idea — Deep Evaluation

For ideas that may justify substantial research time. It guides evaluation of:

- problem and potential impact
- novelty and competitive landscape
- assumption analysis
- earliest go/no-go experiment
- fixed vs. floating parameters
- decision tree and pivots
- failure modes
- longer-term opportunity

## Principle

> Spend more time choosing the problem before spending years solving it.

The repository is intended to preserve not only successful ideas, but also abandoned directions, failed assumptions, pivots, and reasons for saying no. Over time, that history should become a useful map of how research decisions were made.
